Pilot and handover field guide

Independent candidate proposal for a fictional UK assembly site with an installed ball-transfer deck. Use this guide to rehearse the workshop, the commercial decision and the handover. The durations, thresholds and responsibilities below are proposed for testing. The application contains no customer observations or signed commitments.

Customer proposition

Buyer-facing offer sheet. A manufacturing site can trial a defined support package for a named transfer deck over 90 days. Site operations logs interruptions during agreed shifts. The service team delivers the scoped support and reviews repeat issues with the site. The buyer receives an exception log, a comparison against the baseline, a cost breakdown and a renewal offer. Both parties agree the coverage window, fee, service boundary, evidence rules and maximum credits before committing. Existing ownership and product warranty terms are recorded separately.

Technical scope sheet. Record the site, deck identifier, covered components, operating calendar, support contact, competent service provider, access windows, spare availability and agreed exclusions. Identify upstream stoppages, planned shutdowns and site-wide outages in a separate category. Reserve disputed events until the two reviewers agree. Route technical work through the site's authorised personnel and procedures. A distributor may support the agreement when account ownership, local cover and escalation are explicit.

Alwayse already describes tailored servicing and component replacement. Its published work with Atlas Copco illustrates why a modular assembly site is a relevant setting. The trial tests whether formal outcome measurement, shared review and revised payment terms justify additional customer and provider effort. Maintenance services · Atlas Copco assembly case

90-day evaluation

Period Activity Responsible owner Completion evidence
Days 1–30 Register the asset and scheduled shifts. Freeze scope, improvement target, cost boundary and capped credit rule. Collect baseline records, including zero events. Site operations lead and service lead Agreed definitions, baseline extract, sample work order and signed measurement plan.
Days 31–60 Deliver the scoped service and collect the same measures. Compare eligible events against work orders. Review omissions, disputes, exceptions and operator concerns weekly. The 30-day baseline and 60-day intervention have unequal observation windows. Use rates with exposure and event counts. Separate comparable shift patterns and report missing coverage. Large changes in production mix, reporting practice, staffing or adjacent equipment require investigation before drawing a conclusion.

Measurement contract

Measure Calculation and handling Source and review
Covered lost time Covered disruption minutes / scheduled operating hours × 100. Use scheduled hours for the covered deck only. Count overlapping stoppage intervals on the same deck once. Site schedule and event log, reconciled weekly by site and service leads.
Relative change in lost-time rate (Baseline rate − trial rate) / baseline rate × 100. With a zero baseline rate, show absolute rates and counts and revisit the target before renewal. Pilot analyst records period, event count, exposure and exclusions for each observation.
Log completeness Signed deck-shift records / scheduled deck-shifts × 100 per period. A signed zero-event record is complete. Shift lead checks missing entries weekly. Illustrative threshold of at least 90% in both periods.
Response attainment Eligible requests acknowledged inside the agreed window / eligible requests × 100. Agree start, stop and service hours before launch. Support timestamps and service calendar, reviewed with the site owner. Zero eligible requests gives no rate.
Customer value Agreed avoided disruption value and any other evidenced savings less net fees and retained customer costs, compared on the same basis. Finance and buyer reconcile to the commercial model. Compare low and high disruption valuations.
Provider contribution Fees less service credits and attributable service costs using the agreed delivery boundary. Show revenue and costs separately. Service and finance own work orders, parts, travel and cost assumptions. This contribution excludes corporate overhead.
Environmental indicators Service journeys and kilometres; units installed, recovered and disposed of per covered 100 operating hours. Report counts and exposure. Service lead checks visit and parts logs. Discuss repairability and batching without assigning unverified carbon savings.

Record unavailable denominators as unknown. A measured reduction in downtime on an installed assembly line could reflect other process changes. Isolate those changes in the exception notes and discuss their potential direction.

Renewal and exception ownership

Go authorises a scoped next offer subject to real customer approval. The exercise requires all five review gates, a selected service offer with positive expected net customer value and positive expected provider contribution, and a written rationale. In practice, both parties review actual evidence, downside exposure, delivery conditions, consent and contractual terms. No checkbox confirms that fieldwork occurred.

Hold identifies a recoverable gap. For example, a missing night-shift baseline needs an owner, a new observation window and a next review date. Keep the contract unsigned until finance and operations confirm the revision.

Stop closes the trial for an unresolved critical concern, a buyer's rejection or a delivery obligation the provider cannot responsibly support. Record why, close outstanding actions through their owners and arrange the agreed exit or return to existing service arrangements. An operational safety concern goes directly to the site's authorised supervisor irrespective of the review date.

Route to market and transfer

  1. Qualify the first site. Target a domestic assembly customer with an identifiable installed transfer platform, a recurring handling concern and access to operating records. Interview the operator, maintenance lead and budget holder. Reject sites whose service needs or coverage the team cannot support.
  2. Agree the channel. Start with the existing account owner. Where a distributor serves the site, hold joint discovery and define referral, revenue share, service ownership and escalation. Alwayse publishes distributor training and technical support as current capabilities. Distributor programme
  3. Present the offer. Sales walks through the scope sheet, response window, two example events, the draft price, exclusions, maximum credit and review date. Finance independently recomputes the customer and provider cases; service operations signs capacity. Capture objections and version the offer.
  4. Run and review. Use the 90-day method. The account lead tracks qualified accounts, signed pilots, review invitations and paid renewals. Report each conversion with its denominator, and report no rate when the denominator is zero. These measures are proposed; the prototype contains no sales pipeline.
  5. Replicate. When the first buyer is willing to renew, test the same package with a second qualifying site. Reopen the assumptions for operating mix, geography, response cover and spare usage. The sponsor decides whether to invest in repeat delivery.

Training exercise. Allocate 45 minutes. Give sales, engineering and the site representative a fictional confirmed incident, disputed upstream event and missed acknowledgement. Sales explains charges and exclusions in plain language. The service team classifies all three cases, records the owner and demonstrates escalation. Finance reconciles the charge and maximum credit. The nominated trainer observes one full cycle, teaches it back to a deputy and repeats any incorrect step. Record who can run each task independently and revisit gaps after 30 days.

Design-for-service loop. The service owner opens a design issue for repeat identification, accessibility or spares problems observed in the trial. Engineering adds a design-review question and a verifiable serviceability test for a future product revision. Agree ownership, capture the engineering disposition and feed it back to the site, including a reason when no change is adopted.

Post-KTP ownership. The service manager owns the protocol and monthly exception review, finance owns cost inputs and Sales owns renewal. Engineering owns design changes. A deputy covers each role. Handover acceptance requires a complete unaided mock renewal, documentation retrievable from the firm's chosen shared system, and an agreed date for the next review.

Prospective academic output

Working title
Testing the renewal case for a measured support offer on installed ball-transfer equipment.

Protocol abstract
A manufacturing customer can purchase support without knowing whether a differently packaged service creates enough value to renew. This proposed single-site study examines a scoped lifecycle offer for an installed ball-transfer deck. It combines a prospective baseline and intervention with reviewed event logs, service costs and interviews across shifts. The research team would compare interruption rates on matched shifts and investigate discrepancies between modelled value and stated willingness to renew. The planned output is a documented explanation of the conditions under which customer value and delivery economics align. External validity and causal attribution would remain limited until the method is repeated across sites.

Analysis plan. Fix the period, case boundary, eligibility and outcome measure before data collection. Plot weekly rates with operating hours, counts and missing entries. If feasible, compare a similar deck that continues under its existing arrangement and document why it is comparable. Inspect demand mix and critical operational changes. Compare the conclusions including and excluding disputed events. Code interview notes by role and topic, triangulate operator and buyer accounts with work orders, and preserve negative cases. Record any change to the analysis plan. Report descriptive effects and uncertainty; a short uncontrolled study cannot establish the offer caused a change.

Contribution and dissemination. The associate drafts a protocol and field report. An academic supervisor reviews the method, the business sponsor checks confidential content, and participants check attributed material under the agreed permissions. Suitable outputs are an anonymised industry case note and, subject to sufficient evidence and approval, a conference paper on delivery-capability and renewal trade-offs. A literature entry point is Bigdeli and Baines' work on organisational transformation towards servitization. Aston Research Explorer

Responsible practice and development

Duty Practice and owner Evidence to retain in a real project
Health, safety and wellbeing Site supervisor owns observation boundaries and access. The associate offers a confidential way to raise concerns and reasonable timings across shifts. Agreed observation scope, escalation contact and concern disposition.
Fairness, trust and inclusion Invite operators across roles and shifts, offer accessible materials, obtain permission and distinguish observations from disputed interpretations. Report team-level patterns without ranking named workers. Participation coverage, consent record held by the authorised team and a decision trail for excluded evidence.
Environmental sustainability Engineering reviews repair and replace decisions. Service operations monitors visits, distance and disposed parts alongside service outcomes. Parts and visit logs with units and denominators. Evaluate trade-offs between urgent visits and consolidated trips.
Professional development In month one, agree learning goals with the academic and business leads. Practise interview facilitation, service pricing and service operations. Apply one learned technique per phase and reflect at supervision. Training record, revised interview guide or model, supervisor review and a follow-up development goal.
